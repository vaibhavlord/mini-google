How Search works

A small search engine powered by the PageRank algorithm.

01 / INDEX

Start with six pages.

We store only the six supplied URLs, their exact text, and their outgoing links. There’s no crawling and no outside search service.

02 / CONNECT

Let the links vote.

Each page starts with an equal score of 1/6. At every iteration, a page distributes 85% of its score equally across its outgoing links.

03 / SEARCH

Find. Then rank.

We match every word in your query against each page’s text and URL, ignoring case. Matching pages appear in descending PageRank order.

THE PAGERANK FORMULA

PR(i) = (1 − d) / N + d × Σ PR(j) / L(j)

j is a page linking to i. L(j) is its number of outgoing links. N = 6 and d = 0.85.

The 15% random-jump probability gives every page a baseline score. We stop when the total score change is less than 10⁻¹².

WHY REPEATING “PIZZA” DOESN’T WIN

Popularity ≠ repetition.

Page 6 says “pizza” ten times, but no page links to it. Its PageRank stays at the baseline of 0.025. Page 2 earns the highest score through its incoming links, so it appears first for “pizza”.

A NOTE ON RELEVANCE

The words still matter.

PageRank measures the importance of a page in the whole graph. It doesn’t measure query relevance. We first filter by your search words, then use PageRank to order the matches. Repeated words never increase the score.
